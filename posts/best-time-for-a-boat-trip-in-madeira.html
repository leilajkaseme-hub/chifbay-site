Best Time for a Boat Trip in Madeira

27 August 20266 min readFunchal · Madeira

There are really only three slots to choose from off Funchal, and each one gives you a different coast. Here's what actually changes between morning, afternoon and sunset — so you book the one that matches what you came for.

What's the best time of day for a boat trip in Madeira?

It depends on whether swimming or light matters more to you. The 10:00–13:00 morning slot has the calmest water of the day and the clearest photos of the coast. The 14:00–17:00 afternoon slot has the warmest sea for swimming. And the sunset departure from 18:00 trades swimming for the best colour of the day on the cliffs. All three run the same stretch of coast — Câmara de Lobos, Cabo Girão and beyond — just at different hours.

Is the sea calmer in the morning or the afternoon?

Usually the morning. Madeira's south coast works like most Atlantic islands: as the land heats up through the day it pulls in a sea breeze, so wind and light chop tend to build from early afternoon onward. A 10:00 start typically finds the flattest water of the day, which matters more than people expect — flat water means easier swimming, steadier drone shots, and a smoother ride past Cabo Girão. It's rarely rough enough to matter either way, but if calm water is your priority, book the morning.

What does the morning slot (10:00–13:00) actually look like?

It's the same route as the afternoon: Câmara de Lobos, then Cabo Girão with a drone pass, then swim stops at Fajã dos Padres and Ribeira Brava before a fast run back to Funchal. Mornings tend to have the sharpest visibility in the water and the softest light for photos, since the sun hasn't reached its harshest midday angle yet. Food and drinks are aboard either way, so it's really a question of light and calm water versus a slightly warmer sea.

What does the afternoon slot (14:00–17:00) actually look like?

Same coastline, warmer water. By mid-afternoon the sea around Fajã dos Padres and Ribeira Brava has had several extra hours of sun on it, so it's typically the most comfortable time to jump in if you're sensitive to cold water. The trade-off is a touch more wind and a slightly harder midday light for the first hour, which settles down as the afternoon goes on. If you extend to the 3-hour option continuing to Ponta do Sol with filmed video, this is the slot for it — the light softens again toward the end just as you're filming.

Why doesn't the Sunset Trip include swimming?

Because the water is genuinely too cold by then. Sea temperature holds fairly steady through the day, but air temperature drops fast once the sun is low, and getting out of the water into cooling evening air isn't pleasant. The Sunset Trip departs at 18:00 and covers the same coast — Câmara de Lobos and Cabo Girão on the 2-hour option, or on to Ribeira Brava for 2h30 — with drone footage, drinks and food aboard, but no swim stops. What you get instead is the best light of the entire day: the basalt cliffs at Cabo Girão turn deep gold as the sun drops, which is worth the trade for most people.

Should you book the 2h30 or the 3-hour Day Trip?

Book the 3-hour option if you want the trip filmed. Both cover Câmara de Lobos, Cabo Girão and swim stops at Fajã dos Padres and Ribeira Brava — the extra 30 minutes on the €600 option continues on to Ponta do Sol and comes with a filmed video of the day, which is worth it if you want more than phone photos to take home. If your priority is simply the swimming and the cliffs without the extra stretch of coast, the 2h30 option at €500 covers everything that matters.

Should you book the 2-hour or 2h30 Sunset Trip?

It comes down to how far along the coast you want to go before turning back. The 2-hour option turns at Cabo Girão for €400; the 2h30 option continues to Ribeira Brava for €500. Both catch the sunset itself — Madeira's evening light lasts a while as it moves across the sky — so the extra 30 minutes is really about seeing more coastline rather than more sunset.

Does the time of year change the answer?

Yes, mostly for swimming. In summer, June through September, the sea is at its warmest and any slot works comfortably for a swim. In spring and autumn the water is cooler, so the afternoon slot — after the sea has had a full morning of sun — is the more comfortable choice if getting in the water is the whole point of the trip. Light and wind patterns stay fairly consistent year-round, so the morning-versus-afternoon calm-water logic holds in any season.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best time of day for a boat trip in Madeira?
There's no single best time — it depends on what you want. Morning has the calmest water and clearest light, afternoon has the warmest sea for swimming, and sunset has the best colour on the cliffs but no swim stops.

Is the sea calmer in the morning or afternoon in Madeira?
Generally the morning, before the daily sea breeze builds through the afternoon.

Why is there no swimming on a Madeira sunset cruise?
Air temperature drops fast once the sun is low, making it uncomfortable to get in and out of the water, so sunset trips swap swim stops for drone footage and the evening light instead.

What time should I book for the best photos?
Late morning for the clearest water and coastline shots; the hour before sunset for the most dramatic light on the cliffs themselves.

Does the season change which slot is best?
In summer any slot suits swimming; in spring and autumn the afternoon slot is usually the more comfortable choice once the sea has had a few hours to warm up.
