Festa de Nossa Senhora da Piedade 2026: Caniçal's Fishermen's Festival at Sea

1 September 20266 min readFunchal · Madeira

Once a year, the fishing village of Caniçal hands its patron saint over to the sea. Around two dozen trawlers, dressed for the occasion, carry her statue along the coast to a chapel most visitors never notice — a tradition that has nothing to do with tourism and everything to do with three centuries of Atlantic weather. Here's when it happens in 2026, what the maritime procession actually looks like, and how to see it from Funchal.

When is the Festa de Nossa Senhora da Piedade in 2026?

It falls on the third weekend of September, placing the 2026 edition on 19–20 September in Caniçal, a fishing village at Madeira's eastern tip. The feast honours Nossa Senhora da Piedade — Our Lady of Piety — the parish's patron saint and, specifically, the protector of the fishermen who have worked this stretch of Atlantic coast for generations.

What is the festival, and why do fishermen fund it?

It's a votive feast paid for and organised largely by Caniçal's own fishing community, who traditionally set aside a share of their catch earnings each year to cover it. Caniçal was Madeira's main whaling port until commercial whaling ended in the early 1980s, and tuna fishing remains central to the village today — which is exactly why devotion to a saint associated with protection at sea has stayed so strong here, long after similar traditions faded in other coastal parishes. The festival draws hundreds of visitors from across the island and beyond each year, but it remains, at its core, a promise the village keeps to itself.

Why is the chapel built on an isolated clifftop?

According to local tradition, a group of sailors caught in a storm off this coast, convinced their boat was about to be wrecked on the rocks, vowed that if they survived they would build a chapel in thanks. They kept the promise — the small Capela da Piedade, probably dating to the 17th century, has stood ever since on an isolated hilltop known as Monte Gordo, or Monte da Piedade, overlooking Prainha, one of the very few natural sand beaches on Madeira, close to the start of the Ponta de São Lourenço peninsula. It's a striking, deliberately remote setting, and it's the reason the festival needs boats at all — the chapel was never meant to be easy to reach by land.

What actually happens during the maritime procession?

The weekend's centrepiece is the procissão marítima (maritime procession), and it runs on the Saturday afternoon. From around 3pm, the statue of Nossa Senhora da Piedade is carried on foot from the parish church down to the harbour, where roughly two dozen local fishing trawlers wait, freshly decorated with flags and bunting for the occasion. One boat, chosen by lot among the fleet, is given the honour of carrying the statue itself. The flotilla then sails together along the coast toward Monte da Piedade, beneath the chapel, before the image is eventually returned to the church — while onshore, the rest of the weekend fills out with a Mass, a street fair with local food and wine, and live music running into the evening.

How do you get to Caniçal from Funchal, and can you combine it with a boat trip?

Caniçal sits at Madeira's eastern tip, roughly 30 km from Funchal and about 40–50 minutes by car via the VE1 expressway, close to the start of the Ponta de São Lourenço peninsula. It's well worth planning as its own outing rather than an add-on: Caniçal is well east of the stretch of coastline a private boat from Marina do Funchal actually covers, so it doesn't pair on the same trip as a morning on the water. Instead, treat the day in two halves — a morning private boat trip with Chifbay along the closer south-west coast toward Câmara de Lobos and Cabo Girão, then a dedicated afternoon drive east to Caniçal in time for the harbour to fill up before the procession sails.

What else is worth seeing near Caniçal?

The Museu da Baleia (Whale Museum) is the obvious stop, covering the village's whaling history in detail long before the industry's replacement by whale-watching tourism. Prainha, right beneath the chapel, is one of the only natural sand beaches on the whole island. And the Ponta de São Lourenço peninsula, a short drive further east, offers some of Madeira's starkest, most desert-like coastal walking — a useful way to fill the rest of a day built around the evening's festivities.

The chapel was never meant to be easy to reach by land — that's the whole point of the boats.

The Festa de Nossa Senhora da Piedade isn't staged for an audience, and that's exactly why it's worth the drive — a genuine fishing village keeping a centuries-old promise, played out on the water rather than a stage.

Frequently asked questions

When is the Festa de Nossa Senhora da Piedade in 2026?
19–20 September 2026, the third weekend of the month, in Caniçal.

What is the maritime procession?
A Saturday-afternoon flotilla of around two dozen decorated fishing boats carrying the saint's statue along the coast to the clifftop chapel and back.

Why is the chapel on an isolated clifftop?
Local tradition says it was built by sailors as thanks for surviving a storm, probably in the 17th century, on a hilltop above Prainha beach.
