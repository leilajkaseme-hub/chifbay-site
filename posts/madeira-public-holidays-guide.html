Public Holidays in Madeira 2026: What Closes and How to Plan Around Them

31 August 20267 min readFunchal · Madeira

Madeira runs on the same national calendar as mainland Portugal, plus two dates that belong to the island alone — and none of them should catch a visitor off guard if you know what actually closes.

What public holidays does Madeira observe in 2026?

Madeira observes Portugal's 13 national public holidays. In 2026 the dates are: 1 January (New Year's Day), 3 April (Good Friday), 25 April (Freedom Day), 1 May (Labour Day), 4 June (Corpus Christi), 10 June (Portugal Day), 15 August (Assumption Day), 5 October (Republic Day), 1 November (All Saints' Day), 1 December (Restoration of Independence), 8 December (Immaculate Conception) and 25 December (Christmas Day). Because these are civil holidays, banks, public offices, schools and most shops close on each of them.

Which holidays are unique to Madeira, not mainland Portugal?

Two: Madeira Autonomy Day on 2 April, marking the 1976 constitution that gave the archipelago political self-government, and Madeira Day (Dia da Região) on 1 July, commemorating the Portuguese landing at Machico in 1419. Both are regional public holidays, observed island-wide, and neither applies on the mainland or in the Azores. Funchal also has its own municipal holiday, Funchal City Day, on 21 August, which affects the capital specifically rather than the whole island.

What actually closes on a public holiday in Madeira?

Banks, government offices, post offices, most pharmacies (beyond the rota system) and many independent shops close for the day. Large supermarkets often trade shortened hours rather than closing outright, and public transport typically runs a reduced, Sunday-style timetable. If you need cash, a courier, or an official errand done, a public holiday is the one day to plan around.

Do shops and restaurants close in Funchal on holidays?

No — tourism keeps working. Restaurants, cafés, hotels, the Mercado dos Lavradores and the Zona Velha stay open on their normal schedule, since the island's visitor economy doesn't pause for a bank holiday. Boat trips, taxis, car rentals and the main attractions all run as usual, so a public holiday rarely changes anything about a visitor's itinerary — if anything, Funchal tends to feel livelier, with locals out and about instead of at work.

Which 2026 holidays create a long weekend worth planning around?

Portugal doesn't shift a holiday to the nearest weekday if it lands on a Saturday or Sunday — it's simply absorbed into the weekend. What does create a long weekend is a holiday that falls on a Tuesday or Thursday, when many locals take the adjoining Monday or Friday off (a practice known as making a "ponte", or bridge). In 2026, Madeira Day on Wednesday 1 July sits close to the weekend, and Republic Day on Monday 5 October already extends the weekend on its own. Assumption Day on Saturday 15 August 2026 falls on a weekend and won't add an extra day off.

Is a boat trip a good option on a public holiday?

Yes — arguably better than on an ordinary day. With offices and many shops closed, the coast road and Funchal's viewpoints see more local traffic, while the water stays exactly as calm and uncrowded as any other morning. Madeira Day in particular is worth building a trip around: Funchal's bay fills with fireworks and free concerts after dark, and the daytime hours are free for the coast. A private boat trip with Chifbay runs on public holidays just like any other day — Câmara de Lobos, Cabo Girão and a swim stop at Fajã dos Padres on the Day Trip, or the coast at golden hour on the Sunset Trip.

What about Carnival — is it a public holiday?

Carnival Tuesday (17 February 2026) isn't a mandatory national holiday, but Madeira treats it as one by custom. Schools close, many offices give the day off, and Funchal holds its main parade along Avenida do Mar that afternoon and evening. Shrove Monday sometimes closes early as well, so if you're visiting in mid-February it's worth checking a business's hours in advance rather than assuming it's open.

None of Madeira's holidays are a reason to avoid a date — they're mostly a reason to be in Funchal that evening instead of somewhere quieter.

The short version: check the calendar if you need a bank or a government office, and ignore it entirely if you're here for the coast, the food or the sea. The island's tourism side simply doesn't take the day off.
